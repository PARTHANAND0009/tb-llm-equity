Clinical case:
A 12-year-old girl helps her mother with household chores and childcare for younger siblings; she presents after roughly six weeks of low-grade fever and cough, having been screened opportunistically when her mother brought a younger sibling in for a routine immunization visit and mentioned the daughter had 'not been herself.' She denies major weight loss and her growth has otherwise tracked normally. No known TB contact in the household, no prior TB treatment. Chest X-ray shows a right-sided pleural-based opacity with hilar prominence, no cavitation. Two attempts at sputum induction were unsuccessful due to poor cooperation, and a subsequent gastric aspirate was negative on both smear and molecular testing. No HIV risk factors, no suspicion of drug resistance. Exam: mild tachypnea, dullness to percussion at the right base, no peripheral lymphadenopathy.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
