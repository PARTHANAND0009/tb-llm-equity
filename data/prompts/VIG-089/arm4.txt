Clinical case:
A 6-year-old boy is brought in by his mother after about three weeks of intermittent abdominal pain, low-grade fevers, and reduced appetite. She initially thought it was a recurring stomach bug going around his class. He has become noticeably less active. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. Abdominal ultrasound shows mesenteric lymphadenopathy with a small amount of free fluid. Ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration of a mesenteric node was performed and sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: mild diffuse abdominal tenderness, no guarding, no palpable mass, no peripheral lymphadenopathy.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
