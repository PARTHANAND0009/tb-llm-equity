Clinical case:
A 5-year-old boy is brought in by his mother after she noticed a firm, gradually enlarging lump in his armpit over about three weeks. It has not been painful. She initially assumed it was related to a vaccination given some time ago. He has had occasional low-grade fevers. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. Fine-needle aspiration of the axillary node was performed and sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: firm, non-tender 2 cm left axillary lymph node, no overlying skin changes, no other lymphadenopathy, lungs clear.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
