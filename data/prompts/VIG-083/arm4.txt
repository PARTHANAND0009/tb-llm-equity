Clinical case:
A 37-year-old day laborer presents with about nine weeks of progressive abdominal swelling and mild diffuse discomfort, which he attributed to weight gain from a recent change in diet. He has had intermittent low-grade fevers and reduced appetite. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Abdominal exam and imaging reveal moderate ascites with peritoneal thickening. Diagnostic paracentesis was performed; ascitic fluid was sent for molecular testing (along with biochemical and ADA testing), which returned positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: distended, mildly tender abdomen with shifting dullness, no peripheral edema, no jaundice.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
