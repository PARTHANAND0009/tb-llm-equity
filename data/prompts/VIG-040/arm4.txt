Clinical case:
A 72-year-old woman who travels between seasonal work sites with extended family presents after more than three months of right-sided chest discomfort and progressive breathlessness, which the group attributed to 'the damp weather' at her most recent work site. She only agreed to be seen when she became too breathless to continue working. She has had low-grade fevers she hadn't mentioned. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Chest imaging and exam reveal a large right-sided pleural effusion. Thoracentesis was performed; pleural fluid was sent for molecular testing (along with biochemical and ADA testing), which returned positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: decreased breath sounds and dullness to percussion at the right base, no peripheral edema, no lymphadenopathy.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
