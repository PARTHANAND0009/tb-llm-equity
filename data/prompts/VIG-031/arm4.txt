Clinical case:
A 52-year-old man, retired after a long career as a warehouse supervisor, presents with about ten weeks of progressive mid-back pain that he initially attributed to years of heavy lifting on the job. Over-the-counter analgesics have given only partial relief, and over the past two weeks he has noticed mild difficulty with balance that his family has also remarked on. He denies fevers he's noticed but admits he hasn't checked his temperature and has lost some weight without trying. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. MRI of the spine shows destruction of two adjacent vertebral bodies with a paraspinal collection, concerning for spinal TB (Pott's disease). CT-guided aspiration of the paraspinal collection was sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: point tenderness over the mid-thoracic spine, mild gait unsteadiness, no focal motor deficits, no fever.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
