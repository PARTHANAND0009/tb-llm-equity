A 5-year-old girl with known type 1 diabetes, followed regularly at a pediatric endocrinology clinic, was screened for TB symptoms during a routine outreach visit that systematically checks children with diabetes for TB as part of a standing local program; her family had not brought her in specifically for the cough. Her father, who works as a hospital orderly, reports she has had about three weeks of intermittent cough and has needed slightly higher insulin doses than her usual regimen to keep her blood sugars controlled. She has had occasional low-grade fevers. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. Chest X-ray shows a right hilar lymph node pattern suggestive of primary TB. Exam: well-appearing overall, mild right-sided crackles, no respiratory distress, blood glucose well-controlled on current regimen.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
