Clinical case:
A 15-year-old girl who works with her family in the fields presents with about six weeks of a painless, gradually enlarging swelling in her left armpit, which she initially hid from her parents out of embarrassment. She has had mild intermittent low-grade fevers she assumed were part of the same seasonal illness going around. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Fine-needle aspiration of the axillary node was performed and sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: firm, non-tender 3 cm left axillary lymph node with early matting, no overlying skin breakdown, no other lymphadenopathy, lungs clear.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
