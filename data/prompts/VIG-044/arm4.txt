Clinical case:
A 74-year-old woman, long retired from factory work, presents after more than three months of intermittent painless blood in her urine, which she and her family assumed was related to her age or 'just something that happens.' She has mild osteoarthritis managed with occasional analgesics. She has had vague lower abdominal discomfort and some unintentional weight loss. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Urinalysis confirms microscopic and intermittent gross hematuria with sterile pyuria; routine bacterial cultures are negative. Imaging shows irregularity and scarring of the right renal collecting system. Urine was sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: mild right flank fullness, no costovertebral angle tenderness, otherwise unremarkable.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
