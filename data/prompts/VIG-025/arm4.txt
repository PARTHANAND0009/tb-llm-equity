Clinical case:
An 8-year-old boy was identified during a school-based TB screening program that periodically checks students in the area for symptoms, part of a standing outreach activity; his teacher had noted he seemed unusually tired in recent weeks but hadn't flagged a specific complaint. On questioning, his mother reports about three weeks of intermittent low-grade fever and a mild cough she hadn't thought worth a clinic visit. He has no significant past medical history and his growth has been age-appropriate. No known TB contact in the household. Chest X-ray shows right paratracheal lymphadenopathy without cavitation or miliary pattern. Sputum could not be produced despite coaching, and a gastric aspirate was negative on smear and molecular testing. No HIV risk factors, no suspicion of drug resistance. Exam: well-appearing, no respiratory distress, no palpable lymphadenopathy outside the chest findings on imaging.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
