Clinical case:
A 6-year-old boy is brought in for a persistent sore on the side of his neck that has not healed over about three weeks, despite his mother cleaning it daily and applying an antiseptic ointment. It started as a small lump that later broke down into a shallow ulcer with intermittent thin discharge. He has a history of mild eczema, and his mother initially thought this was simply another eczema flare in an unusual location. He has had occasional low-grade fevers. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. A swab and biopsy of the sinus tract were obtained; tissue was sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: a 1.5 cm shallow ulcer with undermined edges over the left neck, connecting to a firm underlying lymph node, mild surrounding eczema elsewhere on his arms.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
