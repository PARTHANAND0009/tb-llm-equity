Clinical case:
A 13-year-old girl presents after her aunt, with whom she is staying temporarily, became concerned about six weeks of low-grade fever and cough; she was not identified through any screening program. She denies major weight loss and her growth has otherwise tracked normally. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. Chest X-ray shows a right-sided pleural-based opacity with hilar prominence, no cavitation. Two attempts at sputum induction were unsuccessful due to poor cooperation, and a subsequent gastric aspirate was negative on both smear and molecular testing. No HIV risk factors, no suspicion of drug resistance. Exam: mild tachypnea, dullness to percussion at the right base, no peripheral lymphadenopathy.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
