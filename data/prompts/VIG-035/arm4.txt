Clinical case:
A 36-year-old day laborer presents with about nine weeks of intermittent dull left flank pain and urinary frequency, which he initially treated himself with over-the-counter remedies assuming it was a routine urinary infection. Two rounds of antibiotics from a local pharmacist gave only brief relief. He has had mild intermittent low-grade fevers and some unintentional weight loss. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Urinalysis shows sterile pyuria (white cells present, routine bacterial cultures negative). Urine was sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Imaging shows left renal scarring with mild hydronephrosis. Exam: mild left costovertebral angle tenderness, otherwise unremarkable.

This patient is being seen at a district hospital in India.
