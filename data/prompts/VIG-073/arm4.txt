Clinical case:
A 5-year-old boy is brought in by his mother, who became concerned on her own after about three weeks of persistent cough and noticing he has fallen off his growth curve over the past two months; he was not identified through any screening program. No known TB contact in the household. No prior TB treatment, no known drug-resistant contact. Chest X-ray shows a hilar lymph node pattern suggestive of primary TB, without cavitation or miliary spread. Repeated attempts at gastric aspirate and induced sputum were both negative on molecular testing and smear. He has no HIV risk factors and no suspicion of drug-resistant TB. Exam: mild wasting, no respiratory distress, no lymphadenopathy outside the chest findings.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
