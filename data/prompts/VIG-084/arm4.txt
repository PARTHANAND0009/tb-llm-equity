Clinical case:
A 61-year-old woman with longstanding, poorly controlled diabetes presents after more than three months of progressive breathlessness and vague chest discomfort, which she and her family attributed to her heart 'acting up given her sugar problems.' She has had low-grade fevers she hadn't mentioned. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. Echocardiogram reveals a moderate pericardial effusion without tamponade physiology at this time. Pericardiocentesis was performed; pericardial fluid was sent for molecular testing (along with biochemical and ADA testing), which returned positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: muffled heart sounds, mild jugular venous distension, no peripheral edema.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
