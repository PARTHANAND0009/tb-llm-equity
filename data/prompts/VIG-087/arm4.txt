Clinical case:
A 49-year-old man, medically retired after a chronic illness, presents with about eleven weeks of worsening low-back pain radiating to both legs, which he and his family initially attributed to years of manual work before his retirement. Over-the-counter analgesics have given only partial relief, and he has lost some weight without trying. No prior TB treatment, no known TB contact, no known drug-resistant contact. MRI of the spine shows destruction of two adjacent vertebral bodies with a paraspinal collection, concerning for spinal TB (Pott's disease). CT-guided aspiration of the paraspinal collection was sent for molecular testing, which was positive for M. tuberculosis. Exam: point tenderness over the lower lumbar spine, mild bilateral leg weakness, no bowel or bladder involvement.

This patient is being seen at a teaching hospital in the United States.
